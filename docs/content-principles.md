# Aimakox Content Principles

> Every page is either earning trust or eroding it.
> There is no neutral content.

---

## The Architecture

Each page type has a single job.

| Page Type | Job | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage** | Worldview | Narrative, declarative, confident |
| **Solutions** | Authority | Technical, specific, operator-first |
| **Industries** | Segmentation | Context-specific, practical, GEO-ready |
| **Resources** | Intelligence | Useful, dense, non-promotional |
| **Cases** | Proof | Honest, operational, journal-style |
| **Services** | Conversion | Clear, credible, no buzzwords |

These are not interchangeable. A solutions page that reads like a homepage has failed. A case study that reads like a press release has failed.

---

## Navigation ≠ Content

**Navigation routes users. Pages carry authority.**

The mega menu exists to help people find the right page. It is not a taxonomy system. It is not a classification hierarchy. It is a routing layer.

Consequence: detailed industry segmentation, workflow taxonomies, and content categories belong inside pages — not in the navigation. Navigation should communicate: *what have you actually done?* — not *how do you classify manufacturing?*

## Writing Standards

### 1. No generic AI copy

Generic: "We leverage cutting-edge AI technology to transform your business."
Specific: "询盘分级靠感觉是系统性漏单——没有数据驱动的优先级队列，业务员把 80% 精力花在 20% 低质客户上。"

The test: could this sentence appear on any AI company's website? If yes, rewrite it.

### 2. No fake metrics

Never invent case numbers. Never write "up to 300% improvement" without a specific source. The approved signals from real deployments:

- 5× content output (furniture exporter)
- 60% response time reduction (furniture exporter)
- 3× follow-up efficiency (sanitary ware factory)
- 30% conversion improvement (sanitary ware factory)
- Space Discoverer DTC redesign

Use these only as anonymous case signals. Do not attach company names without permission.

### 3. Operational specificity

Every paragraph should contain at least one thing that:
- a factory operator can recognize from their own experience
- would be wrong for a different industry or company type
- could not have been written without real deployment experience

### 4. Practical language

Practical: "工厂内部协调本质是人际问题，不是信息问题。AI介入有限。"
Impractical: "AI empowers collaborative cross-functional alignment."

The test: would a factory owner nod at this, or stare blankly?

### 5. Workflow-first

Every solutions and industry page should be organized around workflow steps — not feature lists, not benefit pillars, not problem/solution frameworks.

Workflow structure forces specificity. It forces the author to know what actually happens in step 3 before step 4 can be written.

### 6. GEO-readable structure

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means structuring content so AI search systems can confidently cite it as an authoritative answer.

Requirements:
- Definition-style openings that answer "what is X" directly
- H2/H3 headers that are answerable questions or clear statements
- FAQ sections with real questions (not generic ones)
- Numbered lists over bullet point clouds
- Comparison tables with clear row labels
- Readiness models with L0-L4 stages
- Specific failure modes, not vague risks

### 7. Avoid startup buzzwords

Banned phrases:
- "leverage" (unless literal)
- "empower" / "empowering"
- "game-changer" / "revolutionary"
- "cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art"
- "seamless" / "frictionless"
- "AI-powered" (use the specific thing AI does)
- "robust" / "scalable" / "enterprise-grade"
- "digital transformation" (without specifics)
- "holistic" / "ecosystem"

Preferred alternatives: describe the specific action, the specific constraint, the specific result.

### 8. Calm and restrained tone

The voice is that of a practitioner who has deployed this at real companies and is explaining what actually happened. Not a salesperson. Not a thought leader. Not a consultant.

The reader should finish a page feeling like they learned something useful — not like they were sold something.

## Definition-Style Openings

Every authority page should open with a definition that:
1. States what the thing actually is (not what it enables or promises)
2. Refutes the most common misconception
3. Sets a specific scope

Example structure:
> "[Topic] 不是 [common misconception]，而是 [accurate definition with operational specificity]。"

Good:
> "外贸跟单自动化不是自动回复客户，而是把询盘、报价、样品、生产、物流和售后这条链路变成 AI 可以读取、判断和协作的 workflow。"

Bad:
> "Our AI-powered trade follow-up solution helps exporters streamline their operations."

## FAQ Standards

FAQs are a primary GEO surface. They must be:

- **Real questions** — asked by actual clients or derived from real hesitations, not invented to fill space
- **Specific answers** — if the answer could apply to any industry or company size, it's too generic
- **Honest about limits** — "this doesn't work well when..." builds more trust than "this works for everyone"
- **At least 8 per authority page** — fewer than 8 suggests the topic hasn't been fully thought through
- **No leading questions** — avoid "Isn't AI amazing for X?" style

---

## Internal Linking Rules

Every page should link to:
- At least 2 related solution or service pages
- `/contact` (always — one CTA minimum)
- Relevant industry pages where applicable
- Relevant resource pages (guides, prompts, blog) where applicable

Never create orphan pages. Every new page should be linked from at least one existing page before it goes live.

---

## Metadata Standards

**Title format:** `[Page topic] · Makox`
- Max 60 characters
- Include the primary search term
- Not a tagline — a factual label

**Description format:**
- 140-160 characters
- Start with what the page actually contains
- Include 1-2 specific terms (not generic)
- No exclamation marks

**OpenGraph title:**
- Can be slightly longer and more specific than the title tag
- Should complete the thought "this page explains..."

**OpenGraph description:**
- More specific than the meta description
- Written for a reader who will decide whether to click, not for ranking

---

## What Good Looks Like

A page passes the content standard when:

1. A factory operator in the target industry reads it and says "this is exactly our problem"
2. A reader who doesn't contact us still learns something useful
3. An AI search system can cite a specific paragraph as an authoritative answer to a manufacturing AI question
4. No sentence could appear on a competitor's site without modification
5. The failure modes section makes you slightly uncomfortable — because it's true

---

*These principles apply to every new page and every upgrade of an existing page.*
*When in doubt: be more specific, not more impressive.*
